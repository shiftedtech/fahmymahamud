## Grammar: Chaptered editorial (adapted)
Why: the content is a career story plus reference material (skills, certs, timeline). The visitor should feel they
read something, not watched a film. Why the others lost:
- Filmic one-shot: needs footage/scrub; no real footage and a recruiter needs to jump around.
- Live surface: there is no product to run; faking one breaks the honesty rule.
- Continuous world: no geography to travel; would bury the facts.
- Typographic poster: too little room for the certifications and timeline.
- Gallery: only one project has a public link; a gallery of four would be thin.
- Split stage: before/after fits the story but not five chapters of reference content.
- Rhythmic cutlist: wrong energy for a hiring manager.

Adaptations forced by his explicit rules (these win over the grammar):
- The title page carries the interactive skyline (explicit request). It is line art, not media, and never under text.
- A static (not fixed) header carries logo.png, because he asked for the logo in the header.
- The close keeps a real "Contact me" button next to the running-text email, because his hero spec names that button.

## Nav: the floor indicator (folio)
A fixed lift floor indicator, in the left margin on wide screens and bottom-right on phones. Shows floor number,
chapter name, and a travel arrow while you scroll. Press it and a lift button panel opens; each button jumps to its floor.
Floors: G Lobby · 1 Story · 2 Skills · 3 Projects · 4 Certifications · 5 Experience · 6 Contact.

## Signature move
The lift. The indicator counts floors as you scroll and its button panel is the page's navigation, and on
floor 3 a pair of brushed-steel doors slide open under your scroll to reveal the projects.

## Tell-someone sentence
"It's the site where you ride a lift through his career, and the doors open onto the things he's built."

Adjacent acts carry different feelings.

## The peak
"The lift doors actually opened and his projects were behind them." Act 3. It gets the only pinned act (span 2.2,
the largest), the quietest act before it (skills: plain type, no motion beyond fade), and the one bespoke visual.

## Authored silence
